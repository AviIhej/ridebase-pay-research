# Who buys, what hurts, what they will say

Scope: the 52 Canadian rows in `companies.csv`. Labels: Sourced, Calculated, No source yet, Plan number.

## Who decides at each kind of company

Owner-operated brokerage or family company (for example Casino Taxi, Duffy's Taxi, Hamilton Cab, Kami Cabs).
- The owner or general manager decides. The sites publish a general or customer service inbox and no named finance role. Sourced: contact pages listed in each row's `source_url`.
- Dispatch or driver administration staff are the likely day-to-day contacts. Blue Line Taxi publishes a driver administration inbox. Capital Taxi publishes a driver administration phone line. Sourced: https://bluelinetaxi.com/contact-us/ and https://capitaltaxi.com/contact-page-2/

Driver-owned cooperative (Alberta Co-op Taxi Line, Yellow Cab Vancouver, Co-op Cabs, Taxi Coop Montréal).
- The shareholders or the board decide, and the manager brings the proposal. Sourced: Alberta Co-op Taxi describes itself as driver and employee owned with 70+ shareholders (https://co-optaxi.com/home/index.php/about/). Yellow Cab Vancouver says most of its 325 shareholders are active owner-operators (https://www.yellowcabonline.com/).
- Expect a slower decision than at a family company. No source yet for how any named cooperative votes on a software purchase.

Transit and health contractors (Scarborough City Cab, Co-op Cabs, Royal Taxi, Dignity Transportation, Medi-Van Canada).
- Operations or accounts staff decide. Royal Taxi sends owners to its operations manager (https://www.royaltaxi.ca/join-us/). Diamond Taxi publishes an accounts inbox and a dispatch inbox (https://www.diamondtaxi.ca/).
- Per-trip contract work means many small settlements each week. Scarborough City Cab states it has been a TTC Wheel-Trans contractor since 1995 (https://www.scarborocitycab.com/).

Ride-hailing startup (Apt Rides, R-Hero, Spinr, HOVR).
- The founder or manager decides. Apt Rides publishes a manager inbox (https://aptrides.com/drivers/). R-Hero and Spinr publish support inboxes only. HOVR publishes marketing and investor inboxes only.
- These companies already run a payout flow. Apt Rides offers a bank payout that drivers set to daily or weekly. R-Hero advertises weekly direct deposit. The sale is a replacement of what they run now.

## The pain in their own words

No operator's own words about payroll, bookkeeping or dispatch admin pain were found on pages I read. That is No source yet. The quotes below are operators describing driver pay from the driver side.

- R-Hero: "No 30-day waits. No mystery deductions. Paid every week." Source: https://r-hero.ca/drivers
- HOVR: "Riders pay for it when drivers are charged a large commission." Source: https://www.ridehovr.com/
- Apt Rides: "fair and transparent compensation" for drivers. Source: https://aptrides.com/about-us/
- Checkmate Cabs (a taxi company, on pay timing): "Revenue and non-cash tips are paid weekly." Source: https://www.checkmatecabs.com/careers
- Spinr: "Spinr never takes a share of the fare." Source: https://www.spinr.ca/

These show that startups sell transparent driver pay to recruit. They are a reason to approach them with a statement that shows each driver's figures.

## Rules that create demand

- BC: for ride-hail workers the minimum hourly wage applies to engaged time, and the platform must pay any shortfall when earnings in a pay period fall below the minimum wage times engaged hours. Sourced: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/forms-resources/igm/esr-part-4-section-18-2 (the page text states $20.88 an hour; the rate changes, so check the current figure). This is a top-up calculation that matches the product's hourly guarantee feature. It applies to ride-hail workers, not to taxi drivers. No source yet on whether any BC taxi company in the CSV is affected.

## Objections to expect

1. "Our drivers collect the fare themselves."
   - Owner-operator brokerages may not run a weekly payout at all. Co-op Cabs says its drivers own their vehicles and take cash and card payments (https://co-opcabs.com/about/). Ask first which flows the company settles: corporate accounts, vouchers, chits and transit contracts. Casino Taxi lists corporate accounts and taxi chits (https://www.casinotaxi.ca/).
2. "A cooperative has to vote."
   - See the cooperative decision note above.
3. "Part XX does not apply to us."
   - The Act defines a platform as software that connects sellers to users and excludes work done under an employment relationship (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/page-232.html). A phone-dispatch company may fall outside it. No source yet on where the CRA draws that line. Do not tell a buyer the return is mandatory for them without checking their setup.
4. "The minimum price is too high for us."
   - Plan number: $1 per driver paid per month with a $199 minimum (CAD, from the company website).
   - Calculated: a 34-car company such as Kami Cabs pays the minimum, which is $199 / 34 = $5.85 per car per month. A company with 199 or more drivers pays $1 per driver. Criteria: price as stated, driver count from https://kamicabs.com/.
5. "The Interac fee adds up."
   - Plan number: $0.50 per Interac payout sent by Ridebase.
   - Calculated: 350 drivers each paid once a week by Interac cost 350 x $0.50 = $175 per weekly run, or about $758 a month at 52 runs a year divided by 12. Criteria: Casino Taxi's 350 drivers from https://www.casinotaxi.ca/, every payout sent by Ridebase, one payout per driver per week. Payouts through the operator's own bank cost $0 according to the company website.
6. "Our spreadsheet works."
   - Shadow mode runs next to the sheet and is free for two months (company website). It answers this without asking the buyer to switch first.
7. "Are our drivers employees or contractors?"
   - Several taxi sites describe drivers as independent contractors. Casino Taxi does (https://www.casinotaxi.ca/). The status question is a legal one and No source yet from a primary legal source in this run.
8. "We are not in Canada only."
   - The product runs in Canada today. It does not handle US payouts, and whether it will is undecided. Say that plainly.
